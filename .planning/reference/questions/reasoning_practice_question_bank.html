Cognitive Load Matrix & Distractor Engineering

This section highlights the key theoretical rules used to author distractors, engineer cognitive traps, and construct formal transformations across competitive exam domains.

📐

Spatial & Non-Verbal Rules

  • Mirror Images: Reverses horizontal axis ($NE \to NW$). Preserves vertical elevation.
  • Water Images: Flips vertical axis (Top $\leftrightarrow$ Bottom). Preserves left-right alignment.
  • Paper Folding: Reflection across axis of symmetry. N-folds create $2^N$ mirrored layers.
  • Standard Dice: Sum of opposite faces $= 7$. Adjacent faces can never be opposite.
  • Orthographic Projection: Dashed lines indicate hidden edges/interior voids.
⚖️

Critical & Deductive Rules

  • Negation Test: If negating an assumption collapses the argument, that assumption is necessary.
  • Modus Tollens: $P \to Q$ and $\neg Q$ proves $\neg P$. Reversing without negation is a formal fallacy.
  • Causation vs Correlation: Co-occurrence in time does not prove direct causation without ruling out third-variable confounders.
  • Knights & Knaves: Knights make true statements; Knaves make false ones. Self-referential claims isolate contradictions.
🔢

Numerical & Algorithmic Rules

  • Clock Angle Formula: $\theta = |30H - 5.5M|$. Coincidence occurs at $M = \frac{60H}{11}$.
  • Calendar Repetition: Non-leap years repeat in 6 or 11 years; leap years repeat every 28 years.
  • Cryptarithmetic: Addition carry-over at most significant digit is always $1$.
  • Ranking Formula: $\text{Total} = \text{Rank}_{\text{Top}} + \text{Rank}_{\text{Bottom}} - 1$.

Psychometric Distractor Construction Guidelines

1. Partial Logic Trap Examinee completes step 1 of a multi-step calculation (e.g., finding the East component in direction sense, but forgetting the North component displacement).
2. Directional Inversion Confusing Modus Tollens with Affirming the Consequent, or mistaking mirror reflection for water reflection.
3. Extreme Generalization In Critical Reasoning, selecting options with extreme scope words ("always", "never", "completely immune") that exceed premise bounds.
4. Boundary Off-by-One Errors Overlooking set overlaps in seating arrangements ($N+1$ vs $N-1$) or calendar leap day offsets.
