Psychometric Design, Calibration & Construct Validity

Analogical reasoning tests evaluate an examinee's higher-order cognitive flexibility, fluid intelligence, and structural transfer mechanisms across varied domain contexts. In competitive examination frameworks—such as GMAT, CAT, LSAT, UPSC CSAT, and IBPS—analogies assess the candidate's capacity to isolate relational structures independent of surface content.

1

Structural Mapping Rule

The structural integrity of an analogy relies on identifying a higher-order mapping rule between a base domain pair $(A : B)$ and applying it to a target domain pair $(C : D)$, such that:

f(A) = B  &implies;  f(C) = D

Items prevent guessing by enforcing single-rule determinism ($f(x)$ is unique and non-ambiguous).

2

Item Calibration ($p$-values)

Items are categorized into distinct difficulty parameters based on empirical item response theory targets:

  • Easy ($p \in [0.70, 0.85]$): Single-step taxonomy, simple arithmetic, direct mirror pairs.
  • Medium ($p \in [0.45, 0.69]$): Dual-step transformations ($x^2 - x$, $(d_1+d_2)^2$), domain vocabulary.
  • Hard ($p \in [0.15, 0.44]$): Multi-rule integration (binary counts, decremental shifts, prime factor counts).
3

Distractor Integrity & Error Patterns

Incorrect options (distractors B, C, D) are engineered using validated cognitive error models:

  • Plausible Over-generalization: Represents related domain concepts but violates the precise mapping rule (e.g. selecting Toronto vs Ottawa).
  • Computational Near-Misses: Values reflecting common calculation missteps (e.g. applying $n^2$ instead of $n^2 - n$).
  • Structural Inversions: Reversing rule direction (e.g. $+1$ shift instead of $-1$).
4

Construct Validity & Discrimination

Construct validity mandates that item performance strictly reflects true reasoning ability rather than extraneous variables like regional linguistic bias or specialized niche knowledge. High discrimination indices ($d \ge 0.40$) ensure clear psychometric separation between upper and lower candidate cohorts.
